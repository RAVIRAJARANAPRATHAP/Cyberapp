SCAM TYPE: Digital Arrest Scam

HOW IT WORKS:
Fraudsters call or video-call the victim while impersonating police officers, CBI agents,
customs officials, or telecom authority (TRAI/DoT) representatives. They claim that the
victim's phone number, Aadhaar card, or a courier parcel has been linked to a serious
crime such as drug trafficking, money laundering, or illegal SIM misuse.

The caller then orders the victim to stay on a live video call without disconnecting —
this enforced isolation is what they falsely call a "digital arrest." During this period,
the victim is shown fabricated "arrest warrants," official-looking seals, and sometimes
a staged "police station" background. The fraudsters demand an immediate payment of a
"clearance fee," "bail deposit," or "verification charge" to avoid being physically
arrested. Victims are threatened that any disconnection or call to family will result in
immediate arrest.

TYPICAL SCRIPT USED:
"Your Aadhaar has been used to open a bank account involved in money laundering. You are
under digital arrest. Do not disconnect this call or we will send officers to your home.
To get a stay order you must transfer Rs. X immediately to this account."

RED FLAGS:
- No law in India allows arrest via a phone or video call — "digital arrest" is not a
  legal term or procedure in Indian law.
- Real police, CBI, ED, or TRAI officials never demand money over the phone to "clear"
  your name.
- Genuine law enforcement agencies contact people through official letters or physical
  presence, not WhatsApp or random calls.
- Pressure to stay on call continuously and not contact family is a control tactic, not
  a legal requirement.
- Official-looking documents shown on video can be easily fabricated using free tools.
- Urgency and fear of "immediate arrest" are deliberate manipulation techniques.

WHAT TO DO:
- Hang up immediately, no matter how convincing the caller sounds.
- Do not share OTP, Aadhaar number, bank account details, or any personal information.
- Do not transfer any money, even a small "verification" amount.
- Disconnect the call and reach out to a trusted family member or friend.
- Verify independently: call your nearest official police station directly using a number
  you look up yourself (not one the caller gives you).
- Remember: the moment you feel afraid and pressured, that itself is the biggest red flag.

WHERE TO REPORT:
- National Cybercrime Helpline: 1930 (call, available 24x7)
- Report online at: cybercrime.gov.in
- You can also report to your nearest police station.
- If money was transferred, call your bank immediately to request a transaction freeze.

REAL EXAMPLES:
- In 2024, a retired government employee in Noida lost Rs. 11 lakh after being kept on a
  WhatsApp video call for 36 hours under the pretext of a "digital arrest."
- India's Prime Minister publicly warned citizens about this scam in his Mann Ki Baat
  address in October 2024.
