SCAM TYPE: Fake Relative-in-Trouble / Voice Cloning Scam

HOW IT WORKS:
The victim receives a phone call — often late at night or early morning to maximise
panic — from someone claiming to be a relative (son, daughter, spouse) who is in serious
trouble: arrested, in a road accident, hospitalised, or stranded abroad. The "relative"
begs for an urgent money transfer to cover bail, medical bills, or a travel emergency.

In more sophisticated versions of this scam, fraudsters use AI-based voice cloning tools
to replicate the actual voice of the victim's relative using audio harvested from social
media videos or voice notes. This makes the call sound completely genuine, as the victim
hears what appears to be their own family member's voice crying or speaking in distress.

After the initial "relative" call, a second caller pretending to be a police officer,
doctor, or embassy official confirms the "emergency" and provides bank account or UPI
details to send money to immediately.

TYPICAL SCRIPT USED (voice-cloned version):
[In a familiar voice, tearfully] "Maa/Papa, I'm in big trouble. I had an accident and
the police have taken me. Please send Rs. 50,000 to this number right now, don't tell
anyone, just hurry."

RED FLAGS:
- Unexpected urgent calls from relatives asking for money transfers should always be
  verified independently before sending anything.
- Even if the voice sounds exactly like your relative, hang up and call them back on
  their known saved number to confirm.
- Requests to keep the matter secret from other family members are a control tactic used
  to prevent the victim from getting a second opinion.
- Real embassies and consulates have official processes for helping stranded citizens —
  they do not ask family members to wire money to unknown accounts.
- The combination of emotional distress + urgency + secrecy is the defining pattern of
  this scam.

WHAT TO DO:
- Stay calm. Hang up, then independently call your relative on the number already saved
  in your phone. Do not use any number the caller provides.
- Call another family member or friend who can separately verify whether the relative is
  actually safe.
- Do not transfer any money until you have physically or independently confirmed the
  emergency is real.
- If you cannot reach the relative, call the official number of the institution mentioned
  (hospital, police station, embassy) — not the number given by the caller.
- Remember: a real emergency does not disappear if you take 5 minutes to verify. Scammers
  rely on you not verifying.

WHERE TO REPORT:
- National Cybercrime Helpline: 1930 (call, available 24x7)
- Report online at: cybercrime.gov.in
- Report to your nearest police station, especially if money was transferred.
