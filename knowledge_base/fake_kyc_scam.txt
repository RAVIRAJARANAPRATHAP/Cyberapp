SCAM TYPE: Fake KYC Update Scam (Bank / SMS / Phone Call)

HOW IT WORKS:
Victims receive an SMS, WhatsApp message, or phone call claiming that their bank account,
UPI ID, or mobile wallet (Paytm, PhonePe, etc.) will be blocked or suspended unless they
immediately complete a "KYC update." The message often contains a phone number to call or
a link to click.

When the victim calls back, a fake "bank executive" or "RBI officer" asks them to share
their account number, debit card number, CVV, OTP, or to download a remote-access app
(like AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or Quick Support) to "assist" with the update. Once the
remote-access app is installed, the fraudster silently transfers money from the victim's
account while pretending to help.

In the SMS/link variant, the link leads to a phishing website that looks identical to the
victim's bank's login page. Any credentials entered are captured by the fraudster.

TYPICAL SCRIPT USED (phone version):
"This is [Bank Name] customer care. Your KYC is incomplete and your account will be
blocked in 24 hours. Please share your OTP to complete the update."

TYPICAL SMS TEXT:
"Dear customer, your [Bank] account KYC is expired. Your account will be suspended.
Update now: [fake link]"

RED FLAGS:
- Real banks never ask for OTP, CVV, or full card number over the phone or via a link —
  ever. This is a hard rule with no exceptions.
- KYC updates are done in person at the branch or through the bank's official app/website
  that you navigate to yourself (not through a link someone sends you).
- Any message creating urgency ("blocked in 24 hours," "immediate action required") is
  a pressure tactic.
- The RBI has repeatedly stated it never contacts individual customers directly for KYC.
- Requests to install remote-access or screen-sharing apps are a definitive red flag.
- Sender numbers that are regular mobile numbers (10-digit) rather than the bank's
  official 6-digit short code are suspicious.

WHAT TO DO:
- Never share OTP, CVV, PIN, or passwords with anyone, even if they claim to be from
  your bank, RBI, or any government body.
- Do not click on links in SMS or WhatsApp messages purporting to be from your bank.
- Never install any app at the request of someone who called you.
- If you want to verify whether your KYC is actually due, call the official number on
  the back of your debit card or visit the branch in person.
- If you have already shared details or transferred money, call your bank's fraud helpline
  immediately to block your account and dispute the transaction.

WHERE TO REPORT:
- National Cybercrime Helpline: 1930 (call, available 24x7)
- Report online at: cybercrime.gov.in
- Inform your bank's fraud team immediately so they can attempt a transaction freeze.

SOURCE: RBI Safe Banking awareness guidelines and I4C public advisories (summarized in
own words).
